## 2. How Comparative Rating Works: Personal Lines vs. Commercial Lines

### 2.1 Why Personal Lines Rating is More Mature

Personal lines comparative rating has been established for 15-20+ years. Several structural factors make PL rating fundamentally easier than CL:

**Product Standardization:**
- Personal auto and homeowners policies are highly standardized across carriers (ISO-based forms)
- The core rating variables are well-defined: driver age, vehicle year/make/model, ZIP code, credit score, claims history, property characteristics
- ACORD (Association for Cooperative Operations Research and Development) standardized industry data formats beginning in 1970, and personal lines was the first to benefit
- A standard auto quote requires roughly 30-50 data fields; a standard home quote requires 40-60 fields

**Consumer Price Sensitivity:**
- Personal lines is primarily a price-driven market
- When products became standardized, cost became the #1 decision factor
- This forced agents to adopt speed and comparison tools years before commercial lines

**Limited Underwriting Complexity:**
- Personal auto: relatively binary accept/decline decisions based on MVR, credit, prior insurance
- Homeowners: more complex but still follows standard property/peril rating models
- Commercial lines: every business is unique - 20,000+ NAICS/business class codes, custom endorsements, varying coverage structures

**Rating Algorithm Accessibility:**
- Many PL carriers publish or share their rating algorithms with rater vendors
- Some provide direct API access to their rating engines
- Others allow IVANS-mediated rate exchange
- A few still require website rating (portal automation/screen scraping)

### 2.2 How Real-Time Multi-Carrier Quoting Works (Technical Architecture)

**Three primary methods carriers connect to comparative raters:**

1. **Direct API Integration (Best):**
   - Carrier exposes a RESTful or SOAP API endpoint
   - Rater sends ACORD-formatted quote request with risk data
   - Carrier's rating engine processes in real-time (sub-second)
   - Returns premium, coverage details, eligibility
   - Example: Progressive, Nationwide, Hartford commonly offer API-based rating

2. **IVANS Rating Services (Intermediary):**
   - Cloud-based exchange that sits between raters and carriers
   - Carrier builds one connection to IVANS; IVANS distributes to all participating raters
   - Eliminates need for carrier to maintain separate connections to EZLynx, PL Rating, TurboRater, etc.
   - Uses ACORD-compliant data formats
   - Processes quotes through a standardized exchange layer

3. **Website Rating / Portal Automation (Legacy):**
   - Rater automates data entry into carrier's online quoting portal
   - Essentially RPA/screen-scraping of carrier websites
   - Used by Applied Rater and QuoteRush as primary method
   - Used by EZLynx/PL Rating as fallback for carriers without API/IVANS connections
   - Fragile: breaks when carrier updates their website
   - Slower: must load web pages in background
   - But supports any carrier with an online portal

**Why 15+ Simultaneous Quotes Work in PL:**
- Standardized data model (same fields across carriers)
- Parallel API calls or IVANS exchange requests
- Sub-second carrier response times for API-connected carriers
- 4-10 second total round-trip for full panel quote
- Pre-built underwriting rules filter out ineligible carriers before sending

### 2.3 Why Commercial Lines is Different

- Each commercial risk is unique (restaurant vs. manufacturer vs. tech startup)
- 20,000+ business class codes with different rating factors
- Coverage structures vary wildly (BOP vs. package vs. monoline)
- Endorsements and exclusions are highly customized
- Underwriting requires human judgment, not just algorithmic rating
- Many CL carriers don't offer API-based rating
- Just 24% of independent agencies use a commercial lines rater (vs. near-universal PL rater adoption)
- CL raters (Tarmika, Bold Penguin, EZLynx CL) focus on "small commercial" (BOP, GL) where standardization is more feasible

### 3.2 How Rater-AMS Integration Works

**Data Flow Pattern:**
1. **AMS to Rater (Pre-fill):** Agent selects client in AMS -> client data (name, address, vehicles, drivers, property details) is "bridged" or exported to the rater -> rater pre-fills the quote form
2. **Rater to Carrier:** Rater sends quote request to multiple carriers simultaneously
3. **Carrier to Rater:** Carriers return quotes
4. **Rater to AMS (Write-back):** Selected quote data, premium details, and policy information written back to AMS client record

**Integration Methods:**
- **Native/Embedded:** Epic Quotes within Applied Epic; EZLynx Rating within EZLynx Management; PL Rating within AMS360
- **Bridge/Export:** HawkSoft exports to EZLynx "with a few quick keystrokes"; AMS360 bridges to PL Rating
- **API-based:** Modern integrations use RESTful APIs for real-time data sync
- **ACORD Forms:** Some legacy integrations use ACORD XML format for data exchange

### 3.3 The Role of IVANS

IVANS (now part of Applied Systems since 2016) serves as the central connectivity hub for the insurance industry:

**IVANS Rating Services:**
- Cloud-based exchange connecting carriers to raters
- Carrier builds ONE connection to IVANS -> reaches all participating raters and AMS platforms
- Eliminates 1:1 carrier-to-rater integration overhead
- Grew 50%+ in transaction volume (as reported in 2017; continues to grow)

**IVANS Download:**
- Automated delivery of policy/claims data from carriers to agency AMS
- Processes transactions twice daily
- Post-bind policy data, endorsements, renewals transmitted as ACORD-compliant files
- Supports personal lines: Auto, Dwelling Fire, Flood, Homeowners, Inland Marine, Mobile Home, Package, Umbrella, Watercraft

**IVANS Exchange:**
- Online tool for agencies to review and customize carrier download connections
- Self-service connection management

**Key Tension:** IVANS is owned by Applied Systems, which also owns EZLynx. Vertafore and other competitors must work with an exchange owned by their primary rival. This creates ongoing industry tension about neutrality and access.

## 5. Pain Points That STILL Exist in Personal Lines Quoting

### 5.1 Homeowners Quoting Gaps

**The Core Problem:** Significantly fewer carriers participate in rater-based homeowners quoting compared to auto.

**Evidence from Vertafore PL Rating data:**

| State | Auto Carriers | Home Carriers | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | 45 | 35 | -10 (22% fewer) |
| Florida | 31 | 17 | -14 (45% fewer) |
| Ohio | 43 | 35 | -8 (19% fewer) |
| Texas | ~46 | ~14 | -32 (70% fewer) |

**Why the Gap Exists:**
- Homeowners rating is more complex (property-specific: roof type, year built, square footage, construction, proximity to coast/fire)
- More carrier-specific underwriting rules for property
- Cat-exposed states (FL, TX coast, CA wildfire) have additional complexity
- Many homeowners carriers have manual underwriting requirements
- Property inspection/verification often required before binding
- Regional/specialty homeowners carriers are less likely to invest in API integration

### 5.2 State-Specific Challenges

**Florida Homeowners:**
- The hardest state for comparative homeowners quoting
- Only 17 carriers in PL Rating for homeowners (vs. 31 for auto)
- Many Florida-only carriers (American Integrity, Florida Peninsula, Heritage, Monarch National, Security First) may not participate in all raters
- Citizens (insurer of last resort) requires proof that private market quotes are 20%+ higher
- Recent depopulation initiative moved 541,000+ policies from Citizens to private carriers, creating market churn
- 17+ new carriers entered FL since 2022-2023 reforms, but many are new and may not be in raters yet

**California:**
- Wildfire exposure creates carrier selectivity
- State regulatory environment (Proposition 103) adds complexity
- Some carriers withdraw from specific ZIP codes
- FSC Rater has been the California-specific standard for 30+ years
- Safeco has at times temporarily not supported 3rd-party quoting in CA

**Texas:**
- Severe weather (hail, wind, tornado) creates underwriting complexity
- Only ~14 homeowners carriers in PL Rating despite 60 total carriers
- Many Texas homeowners carriers write through their own portals only

**Ohio:**
- Relatively stable market with good carrier participation
- 43 auto / 35 home carriers is among the better ratios

### 5.3 Data Accuracy Issues with Pre-fill

**The Speed vs. Accuracy Tension:**
Pre-fill data sources (LexisNexis, Verisk, public records) provide instant property and driver data but with known accuracy limitations:

- **Property data varies by geography** - most reliable data source differs by field and region
- **Year built** may be best sourced from public records
- **Roof type/cover** may be more accurately derived from prior claims data
- **Pools, outbuildings** - public records may not reflect recent additions; aerial imagery catches this but not always current
- **Square footage discrepancies** between public records and actual measurements
- **Identity verification gaps** - typos, incomplete applications, potential fraud
- **Underinsurance risk** - inaccurate pre-fill can lead to insufficient coverage amounts

**LexisNexis** addresses some challenges through identity verification to detect fraud and typos. **Verisk** is developing "smart prefill" that cross-references multiple data sources for higher accuracy.

**Agent Impact:** Agents must manually verify and correct pre-filled data, which partially negates the time savings. Inaccurate pre-fill can lead to quote discrepancies that damage client trust.

### 5.4 Quote-to-Bind Workflow Gaps

**The Traditional Problem:**
Historically, comparative raters were quote-only tools. The workflow was:
1. Enter data in rater
2. Get multi-carrier quotes
3. Select best option
4. Navigate to carrier's portal
5. Re-enter or verify data on carrier site
6. Complete underwriting questions
7. Process payment
8. Bind policy
9. Return to AMS and manually enter policy details

Steps 4-8 represent significant friction, requiring platform switching and partial data re-entry.

**Progress Being Made:**
- **Vertafore PL Rating:** Launched inline binding (April 2024) with Acuity Insurance. Agents can bind and issue directly within PL Rating. But still limited to select carriers.
- **EZLynx:** Working on deeper carrier integration to reduce portal switching
- **IVANS Download:** Automates step 9 by downloading policy data back to AMS post-bind

**Remaining Gaps:**
- Inline binding is only available with a few carriers
- Most carriers still require portal visit for final binding
- Payment processing varies by carrier
- Endorsement and mid-term changes still require carrier portal
- No rater offers true "one-click bind" across all carriers

### 5.5 Carrier-Specific Supplemental Questions

**The Problem:** Raters collect a standardized set of data fields sufficient for most carriers. But individual carriers often have proprietary underwriting questions not captured in the standard rater flow:

- Specific roof condition questions (FL carriers)
- Trampoline/dog breed exclusion questions
- Home business use questions
- Swimming pool fencing requirements
- Credit consent and disclosure variations
- Prior insurance verification requirements
- Property inspection scheduling
- Flood zone determination questions
- Wildfire mitigation questions (CA carriers)

**Impact:**
- Agents must answer supplemental questions after selecting a carrier, adding time
- Some supplemental questions change the quote (sometimes significantly)
- Creates a "bait and switch" experience: rater quote differs from final carrier quote
- EZLynx tries to address this by "prefilling fields you need and hiding ones you don't" but carrier-specific questions are inherently non-standardizable

### 5.6 Remarketing/Renewal Quoting Challenges

**Hard Market Impact:**
- During the current hard market, carriers have been WITHDRAWING from raters or providing inaccurate rates within raters to manage capacity
- Steep rate increases at renewal drive massive remarketing volume
- Agencies report being overwhelmed with remarketing requests from upset clients
- Carriers withdrawing from markets create gaps in rater results

**Operational Challenges:**
- Re-quoting existing clients at renewal requires pulling current policy data and re-rating across carriers
- Client data may have changed since initial quote (new drivers, home improvements, claims)
- Volume of remarketing in hard market strains agency staff
- Economics of remarketing are challenging: time-intensive process for uncertain outcome
- Some carriers offer different rates for new business vs. renewal, complicating comparisons

**Rater Capabilities:**
- EZLynx and PL Rating store client data for quick re-quoting
- But data freshness is a concern - stored data may be outdated
- Automated remarketing triggers exist but require clean data
- No rater fully automates the renewal remarketing workflow end-to-end

**Current State:**
- UBI moving from niche to mainstream in 2025-2026
- Telematics-driven programs deliver 10-30% premium discounts for safe drivers
- Progressive's Snapshot, Allstate's Drivewise, State Farm's Drive Safe & Save are category leaders

**Technology Approaches:**
- **Plug-in OBD-II devices:** More accurate but requires physical installation
- **Smartphone-based:** Convenient, no hardware, but accuracy questions (GPS + accelerometer)
- **Connected car data (OEM):** Vehicle manufacturers sharing driving data directly with insurers (growing trend)

**Agent Opportunity:**
- Position as "telematics-forward" advisor
- Help clients understand and optimize their UBI scores
- Younger, tech-savvy demographic attracted to fairer pricing models

**Quoting Challenge:**
- UBI rates require driving data that doesn't exist for new customers
- Initial quote may differ significantly from actual premium after telematics period
- Comparative raters don't fully account for UBI discount potential

### 6.5 Parametric Insurance

**Emerging in Personal Lines:**
- Pays out based on predefined parameter (e.g., hurricane wind speed exceeds threshold) rather than proof of actual loss
- Fills gaps that traditional programs leave, particularly in catastrophic markets
- Growing in FL, TX, CA for weather-related coverage supplements

**Agent Opportunity:**
- Bundle parametric riders with traditional policies for comprehensive protection
- Particularly relevant in cat-exposed states where traditional carriers have gaps

**Quoting Challenge:**
- Not yet integrated into standard comparative raters
- Requires separate quoting and placement processes
- Products are still relatively niche and non-standardized

## 8. Key Takeaways and Strategic Implications

1. **The PL rater market is a duopoly:** EZLynx and PL Rating dominate, with nearly half of agents using one or the other. TurboRater is a strong #3.

2. **Homeowners quoting is the biggest remaining gap:** Across all raters, homeowners carrier participation lags auto by 20-70% depending on state. FL and TX are worst.

3. **Quote-to-bind is the next frontier:** Vertafore's inline binding is a first step, but carrier participation is minimal. The rater that solves full-workflow binding wins.

4. **Hard market creates rater dysfunction:** Carriers withdrawing from raters or providing inaccurate rates undermines the entire comparative rating value proposition.

5. **API-first is winning:** Screen-scraping/portal automation is a declining approach. Carriers investing in APIs get more distribution; those that don't get left behind.

6. **AI will augment, not replace, comparative rating:** AI will make data entry faster and more accurate, predict eligibility, and optimize coverage. But the core multi-carrier comparison function remains essential.

7. **Embedded insurance is a long-term threat:** Independent agents must demonstrate value beyond price comparison, or risk being disintermediated by embedded insurance at point of purchase.

8. **The AMS-rater integration is tightening:** Vendors are creating walled gardens (Applied owns EZLynx + IVANS; Vertafore owns PL Rating + AMS360). Rater-agnostic AMS platforms like HawkSoft offer agencies more flexibility.
