## 3. STATE-SPECIFIC NUANCES

### CALIFORNIA

**Prop 103 Rate Regulation:**
- All rate changes require prior approval from the CA Department of Insurance
- Historically, carriers have struggled to get adequate rate increases approved in a timely manner
- This has caused carriers to restrict new business rather than accept underpriced risk
- Commissioner Lara's Sustainable Insurance Strategy (2024-2025) is attempting to modernize: allowing catastrophe models for the first time, requiring carriers who get rate increases to write in wildfire-distressed areas at 85% of their statewide market share

**Wildfire Exposure & Moratoriums:**
- FAIR Plan policies grew 276% from 2018-2024; now at ~$1.4B in premium (was $87M in 2018)
- Commissioner has issued rolling 1-year moratoriums on non-renewals in wildfire-affected zip codes since 2018
- State Farm non-renewed 72,000+ policies; Allstate paused new business in 2022
- Farmers announced December 2024 they would re-expand CA writings
- Three catastrophe models now approved for carrier use (Verisk, Karen Clark, Moody's)

**FAIR Plan:**
- ~4% of CA residential market (and growing rapidly -- 45% growth in 2024 alone)
- Filed for 35.8% average rate increase in late 2025
- Commissioner pushing to make FAIR Plan temporary, not permanent
- Coverage cap is $3M for residential (expanded from $1.5M)

**Impact on IAs:** CA is the hardest state for independent agents to place homeowners. Limited IA carriers, rate regulation delays, and wildfire restrictions mean agents often must layer FAIR Plan + DIC (Difference in Conditions) policies from surplus lines markets to cover clients.

### FLORIDA

**Hurricane Exposure:**
- FL represents ~$5 trillion in insured coastal property value
- Average FL homeowners premium is among the highest nationally (~$4,200/year average)
- 17+ new carriers entered the market since 2024 (positive trend after years of carrier exits)

**Citizens Property Insurance:**
- State-created insurer of last resort
- Peaked at ~1.4M policies; now ~560,000 (late 2025) thanks to aggressive depopulation
- 18 companies approved to assume 1M+ policies through 2024
- Slide Insurance and Manatee Insurance Exchange took the largest depopulation tranches
- Citizens' rates are being brought closer to actuarial adequacy to discourage use

**Assignment of Benefits (AOB) Reform:**
- SB 2-A (2022 special session) banned AOB for all new residential policies issued on/after Jan 1, 2023
- This was the #1 driver of FL carrier insolvencies; reform is already reducing litigation
- Result: carrier profitability improving, new entrants arriving

**Roof Age Restrictions:**
- Insurers can deny or non-renew based on roof age of 15+ years (even if roof is functional)
- Homeowners can override with certified inspection showing 5+ years remaining useful life
- Citizens has its own roof age eligibility exceptions (Rule 103.11, effective Aug 2024)

**Sinkholes:**
- Primarily a Central FL issue (Pasco, Hernando, Hillsborough counties)
- Sinkhole coverage is optional; catastrophic ground cover collapse is required
- Few carriers offer full sinkhole coverage voluntarily

**Impact on IAs:** FL is actually one of the best states for IAs in homeowners -- most FL-domestic carriers (Universal, Tower Hill, American Integrity, Florida Peninsula, Edison, Heritage) distribute exclusively through IAs. The challenge is financial strength: many FL carriers are rated A- or B++, and carrier insolvencies have been common.

### OHIO

**Competitive Market Dynamics:**
- OH is one of the most competitive IA markets in the country
- Independent agencies control 58% of homeowners and 64.5% of all P&C premium
- IA channel experienced its largest growth in 5 years in 2024 (+0.8%), gaining share from direct writers
- Multiple OH-headquartered carriers: Nationwide, Progressive, Grange, Cincinnati, Westfield, Encova, Ohio Mutual

**Weather/Tornado Exposure:**
- 2024 was devastating: 74 tornadoes (state record), including a May multi-day outbreak producing 165+ tornadoes regionally
- Estimated $6.6B in cost-adjusted damages
- Convective storm losses driving carrier profitability challenges (Erie at 108.5%, Westfield at 102.2%, Ohio Mutual at 115.5%)
- New trend: separate wind/hail deductibles and roof depreciation schedules being introduced

**State Regulations:**
- OH is a competitive-rating state (file-and-use for most lines)
- Relatively carrier-friendly regulatory environment
- No prior-approval requirement for most rate changes
- Homeowners premiums increased 36.4% on average from 2019-2024

**Market Performance:**
- After consecutive years of triple-digit combined ratios (103% in 2022, 115% in 2023), carriers achieved 92% combined ratio in 2024 -- a 23% improvement
- Premiums increased 14% in 2024
- Agent commissions rose to 13.1% from 12.9%
- Carriers not expected to reduce rates to pre-hard market levels nor remove roofing restrictions

**Impact on IAs:** Ohio is the ideal IA state among these four. Deep bench of IA carriers, strong market share, competitive dynamics, and multiple headquartered carriers competing for agent business. The weather challenge is real but manageable with proper carrier diversification.

### TEXAS

**Windstorm/Hail:**
- TX is the #1 hail-loss state in the US
- Severe convective storms drive more losses than hurricanes in most years
- Carriers increasingly adding separate wind/hail deductibles (typically 1-2% of dwelling value)
- Some carriers excluding wind in coastal counties, requiring separate TWIA coverage

**TWIA (Texas Windstorm Insurance Association):**
- Covers 14 first-tier coastal counties where many private carriers won't write wind
- ~230,000+ policies; average residential premium ~$2,480
- TWIA filed for 10% rate increase in 2024 (denied by Commissioner in Oct 2024)
- Rates are ~3% inadequate for residential, ~5% for commercial (improved from 38%/45% inadequacy in 2023)
- Capped at 10% annual increase, 15% per rating class

**File-and-Use Rating:**
- TX uses file-and-use for most personal lines (more flexible than CA's prior-approval)
- Carriers file rates and can use them unless the Commissioner objects within 60 days
- This allows faster rate adequacy adjustments compared to CA
- Over 1,167 insurance groups and 3,107 companies writing in TX (2024)

**Geographic Diversity:**
- North TX: severe hail (DFW metroplex is the most hail-damaged area in the US)
- Gulf Coast: hurricane/wind (TWIA territory)
- Central TX: tornado alley extension
- West TX: lower exposure, more rural
- South TX: proximity to border; unique demographics

**Impact on IAs:** TX is a large, competitive market with good IA options. The key challenge is hail/wind. Travelers, Safeco, Nationwide, and Progressive are the workhorse IA carriers. Homeowners of America is a growing TX-focused IA option. The county mutual structure is unique to TX and can be confusing for out-of-state agents.

## 7. SUMMARY: KEY FINDINGS FOR INDEPENDENT AGENCIES

### The "Big 3" IA Carriers Across All 4 States
1. **Safeco (Liberty Mutual)** -- Home + Auto in CA, FL, OH, TX. The workhorse IA carrier.
2. **Travelers** -- Home + Auto in CA, FL, OH, TX. Best financial strength (A++). Excellent tech.
3. **Progressive** -- Home + Auto in CA, FL, OH, TX. Dual distribution but massive IA presence. Best tech overall.

### State-Specific MVPs for IAs
- **California:** Mercury (CA-native, competitive pricing), Safeco, Travelers
- **Florida:** American Integrity (IA-exclusive, HO only), Tower Hill, Universal; pair with Progressive or Travelers for auto
- **Ohio:** Erie (dominant IA carrier), Cincinnati, Auto-Owners, Grange -- deepest IA bench
- **Texas:** Safeco, Travelers, Progressive; Homeowners of America for TX-focused HO

### Biggest Challenges by State
- **California:** Prop 103 rate delays, wildfire moratoriums, limited IA carrier options
- **Florida:** Carrier financial stability (many A- or B++ rated), no bundling from FL-domestic carriers
- **Ohio:** Severe convective storm losses driving rate increases; some carriers struggling with profitability
- **Texas:** Hail losses in DFW, coastal wind exposure (TWIA), large geographic underwriting variation
