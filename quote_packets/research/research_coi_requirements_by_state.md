## 1. Industry Standard Forms Overview

Before diving into state specifics, these are the core ACORD forms relevant to COI issuance:

| Form | Full Name | Use Case |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **ACORD 25** | Certificate of Liability Insurance | General liability, auto liability, umbrella/excess liability, workers' comp. The most widely used COI form in commercial lines. |
| **ACORD 27** | Evidence of Property Insurance | **Personal lines property** -- used to confirm homeowners/dwelling coverage to mortgage lenders. Shows mortgagee/loss payee designation. |
| **ACORD 28** | Evidence of Commercial Property Insurance | **Commercial property** -- confirms commercial property coverage to lenders, landlords, or other third parties. |

**Critical distinction**: A COI is informational only. It does not amend, alter, or extend coverage. It does not confer contractual rights on the certificate holder. Actual coverage rights require endorsements to the underlying policy (e.g., additional insured endorsements, mortgagee clauses, loss payee provisions).

## 2. California

### 2.1 Personal Lines -- Home Insurance

**Standard Proof of Coverage Documents:**
- **ACORD 27 (Evidence of Property Insurance)**: Industry standard form used to provide evidence of homeowners coverage to mortgage companies. Shows policy number, coverage limits, deductibles, named insured, and mortgagee/loss payee designation.
- **Declarations Page ("Dec Page")**: The first page of the homeowners policy summarizing coverages, limits, deductibles, and premium. Often requested by lenders at closing.
- **Insurance Binder**: Temporary evidence of coverage used between binding and policy issuance; commonly required several days before loan closing.

**Mortgage Company / Lender Requirements:**
- Lenders require dwelling coverage at least equal to the replacement cost of the home (or outstanding loan balance, whichever is less, per lender policy).
- Lender must be listed as **mortgagee** and **lender's loss payable** on the policy.
- Standard mortgagee clause (or "union mortgage clause") giving the lender independent rights under the policy is required.
- Lenders require cancellation/non-renewal notification -- typically 30 days advance written notice.

**California FAIR Plan Considerations:**
- Due to wildfire risk, many California homeowners can only obtain coverage through the **California FAIR Plan** (insurer of last resort).
- The FAIR Plan provides named-peril coverage only (fire, lightning, smoke, internal explosion) -- this alone **does not meet most mortgage lender requirements**.
- Homeowners typically need a supplemental **Difference in Conditions (DIC)** or wraparound policy to cover liability, theft, water damage, and additional living expenses.
- As of March 2025, the FAIR Plan has over 555,000 residential policies in force, up 23% from September 2024.
- **AB 226 (FAIR Plan Stability Act)**: Allows the FAIR Plan to access catastrophe bonds and credit facilities (effective 2025).

**State-Specific Regulatory References:**
- **Cal. Insurance Code, Division 2, Part 6**: Governs fire insurance and property coverage.
- **Cal. Code Regs. Title 10, Section 2670.10**: Certificate of insurance filing requirements for credit insurance -- requires all coverages, exclusions, and limits be set forth in the certificate issued to the certificate holder.
- California does **not** have a standalone COI-specific statute comparable to Texas Chapter 1811 or Ohio Chapter 3938. COI practices are governed by general insurance regulations and industry standards.

### 2.2 Personal Lines -- Auto Insurance

**Standard Proof of Coverage Documents:**
- **Standard Proof of Financial Responsibility Card**: Issued by the insurer per Vehicle Code Section 16028, showing insurer name, policy number, vehicle information, and coverage period.
- **Insurance Policy or Photocopy**: Full policy document or copy.
- **Insurance Binder**: Temporary confirmation of coverage.

**Lienholder Requirements:**
- Lienholders (auto loan providers) require **comprehensive and collision coverage** in addition to liability.
- Lienholder must be listed as **loss payee** on the policy.
- Policy must include notification of cancellation provision to the lienholder.

**Electronic Proof of Insurance:**
- **AB 1708 (2012)**: Amended Vehicle Code Section 16028 and Insurance Code Section 515 to allow drivers to display proof of financial responsibility on a **mobile electronic device**.
- Peace officers may request proof at traffic stops but **cannot stop a vehicle solely to check insurance** (Veh. Code Section 16028(c)).

**Minimum Liability Requirements (effective January 1, 2025 -- SB 1107):**
- **$30,000** bodily injury per person (up from $15,000)
- **$60,000** bodily injury per accident (up from $30,000)
- **$15,000** property damage per accident (up from $5,000)
- Referenced as "30/60/15" -- the first increase since 1967.
- Future increase scheduled for January 1, 2035: 50/100/25.

**State-Specific Forms:**
- California does **not** use SR-22 or FR-44 for DUI convictions. Instead, California uses the **SR-22 (Certificate of Financial Responsibility)** filing for serious violations (DUI, driving without insurance, at-fault uninsured accidents).
- SR-22 must be maintained for 3 years and is filed electronically by the insurer with the CA DMV.

**Key Statutes:**
- **Vehicle Code Section 16020**: All drivers must maintain financial responsibility and carry evidence in the vehicle.
- **Vehicle Code Section 16028**: Proof must be provided to peace officers on demand; electronic display permitted.
- **Vehicle Code Section 16056**: Defines minimum coverage amounts (as amended by SB 1107).

### 2.3 Commercial Lines

**Standard COI Forms:**
- **ACORD 25**: Certificate of Liability Insurance -- used for CGL, commercial auto, umbrella/excess, workers' comp.
- **ACORD 28**: Evidence of Commercial Property Insurance -- used for commercial property coverage.

**Additional Insured Requirements:**
- Standard industry practice; no California-specific statutory mandate beyond contractual requirements.
- Common endorsements: **CG 20 10** (ongoing operations) and **CG 20 37** (completed operations).

**COI Regulatory Framework:**
- California has **no dedicated COI statute** restricting certificate content or imposing penalties for COI misuse.
- General prohibitions against insurance fraud and misrepresentation under the California Insurance Code apply.
- **Cal. Civil Code Section 2782**: Restricts indemnity provisions in construction contracts (void if indemnifying against sole negligence of the indemnitee) but does not directly regulate COI content.
- The California Department of Insurance has not issued specific bulletins on COI practices as of this writing.

## 3. Ohio

### 3.1 Personal Lines -- Home Insurance

**Standard Proof of Coverage Documents:**
- **ACORD 27 (Evidence of Property Insurance)**: Standard form for evidencing homeowners coverage to mortgage companies.
- **Declarations Page**: Summary of policy details; commonly requested by lenders at closing and at renewal.
- **Insurance Binder**: Temporary proof of coverage before policy issuance.

**Mortgage Company / Lender Requirements:**
- Lenders require coverage not exceeding the **replacement cost** of the home and its contents.
- Lender must be designated as **mortgagee** with standard mortgagee clause.
- Proof of insurance (binder or dec page) required several days before closing.
- **Ohio FAIR Plan**: Available for homeowners unable to obtain coverage in the voluntary market (ohiofairplan.com). Covers dwelling fire and allied perils. Mortgage lenders may require supplemental coverage for perils not covered.

**State-Specific Notes:**
- Ohio does not mandate homeowners insurance by statute, but mortgage lenders universally require it contractually.
- No Ohio-specific homeowners COI form requirements beyond standard ACORD forms.

### 3.2 Personal Lines -- Auto Insurance

**Standard Proof of Coverage Documents:**
- **Financial Responsibility Identification Card**: Issued per Ohio Administrative Code Rule 4501:1-2-02.
- Must show: name of insured, name of insurer/surety, effective and expiration dates, and description of covered vehicles (including fleet reference if applicable).

**Electronic Proof of Insurance:**
- **Ohio Senate Bill 255 (effective March 23, 2015)**: Permits proof of financial responsibility via electronic display on smartphones, tablets, and other portable devices.
- Amended OAC Rule 4501:1-2-02 to accept electronic format.

**Lienholder Requirements:**
- Lienholders require comprehensive and collision coverage.
- Lienholder must be listed as loss payee.
- Insurer must provide cancellation notice to the lienholder.

**Minimum Liability Requirements:**
- **$25,000** bodily injury per person
- **$50,000** bodily injury per accident
- **$25,000** property damage per accident
- Referenced as "25/50/25."

**State-Specific Forms:**
- Ohio uses the **SR-22 (Certificate of Financial Responsibility)** for drivers with serious violations (DUI/OVI, driving without insurance).
- SR-22 alternatives: $30,000 deposit with state treasurer, $30,000 surety bond, or certificate of self-insurance (25+ vehicles).

**Key Statutes:**
- **ORC Section 4509.101**: Financial responsibility requirements.
- **ORC Section 4509.45**: Proof of financial responsibility.
- **OAC Rule 4501:1-2-02**: Financial responsibility identification card requirements.

### 3.3 Commercial Lines

**Standard COI Forms:**
- **ACORD 25, 27, 28**: All standard forms in use.

**COI Regulatory Framework -- Ohio Certificates of Insurance Act (ORC Chapter 3938):**

Ohio enacted comprehensive COI legislation in 2016, one of the most detailed in the country.

**Section 3938.01 -- Definitions:**
- "Certificate of insurance" = document prepared or issued by an insurer or licensed agent to verify the existence of property or casualty insurance coverage.

**Section 3938.02 -- Nature of Certificate:**
- A COI is **not** a policy of insurance.
- Does not amend, extend, or alter coverage afforded by the referenced policy.
- Shall not confer new or additional rights beyond what the referenced policy expressly provides.

**Section 3938.03 -- Language Prohibited:**
- A COI shall **not** contain language that is:
  - Unfair, misleading, or deceptive, or that violates public policy.
  - In violation of any law or rule adopted by the Superintendent of Insurance.
- A COI **cannot** guarantee that the referenced insurance policy meets Ohio property or casualty requirements.
- Inclusion of a contract number or policy description is **not** proof of such a guarantee.

**Section 3938.04 -- False Information; Alteration of Policy:**
- Prohibited to issue a COI containing **false or misleading information** about the referenced policy.
- Prohibited to issue a COI that **affirmatively or negatively alters, amends, or extends coverage**.

**Section 3938.05 -- Notice of Cancellation/Nonrenewal:**
- A certificate holder is entitled to notice of cancellation or nonrenewal **only if**:
  - The certificate holder is **named within the policy or endorsement**, AND
  - The policy or endorsement **requires** such notice.
- The terms and timing of notice are governed by the **policy itself** -- they cannot be altered by the COI.

**Section 3938.07 -- Void Certificates:**
- A COI issued in violation of Chapter 3938 is **void**.

**Penalties & Enforcement:**
- Ohio Department of Insurance, Division of Fraud and Enforcement handles complaints.
- Superintendent may issue **cease and desist** orders.
- Civil penalty of up to **$1,000 per violation**.
- Potential **license suspension or revocation** for agents.
- Both agents **and** businesses demanding falsified COIs are liable.

**Effective Date:** March 23, 2016 (House Bill 259, 131st General Assembly).

## 4. Florida

### 4.1 Personal Lines -- Home Insurance

**Standard Proof of Coverage Documents:**
- **ACORD 27 (Evidence of Property Insurance)**: Standard for evidencing coverage to mortgage companies.
- **Declarations Page**: Summary document commonly required at closing and at each renewal.
- **Insurance Binder**: Temporary proof before policy issuance.

**Mortgage Company / Lender Requirements:**
- Standard mortgagee clause and loss payee designation required.
- Coverage must meet replacement cost or loan balance requirements per lender.
- Cancellation/non-renewal notice to the lender -- typically 30 days.

**Florida-Specific Considerations:**
- **Wind/Hurricane Coverage**: Florida Statute 627.712 governs exclusions for windstorm coverage. Many lenders require wind coverage, which may need to be obtained separately in coastal zones.
- **Citizens Property Insurance Corporation**: Florida's insurer of last resort. Citizens requires a **4-Point Inspection** (roof, electrical, plumbing, HVAC) for homes over 20 years old.
- **Wind Mitigation Inspection**: Uses the official **OIR-B1-1802 Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form**. Insurance companies are **required by statute** to offer discounts for wind mitigation features.
- **Flood Insurance**: Federally required for properties in FEMA-designated flood zones; separate NFIP or private flood policy required.
- **Sinkhole Coverage**: Under Fla. Stat. 627.706, insurers must make sinkhole loss coverage available; catastrophic ground cover collapse coverage is mandatory in all policies.

**State-Specific Regulatory References:**
- **Fla. Stat. 627.701-627.7155**: Property insurance contracts, including deductible requirements and coverage mandates.
- **Fla. Stat. 627.712**: Windstorm coverage requirements and exclusions.
- **Florida Office of Insurance Regulation (FLOIR)**: Regulates property insurance forms and rates.

### 4.2 Personal Lines -- Auto Insurance

**Standard Proof of Coverage Documents:**
- **Uniform Proof-of-Purchase Card**: Per Fla. Stat. 320.02(5)(a), insurers must furnish uniform proof-of-purchase cards in paper or electronic format, prescribed by the DHSMV.
- Must include: name of insurance company, coverage identification number, make/year/VIN of insured vehicle.

**Electronic Proof of Insurance:**
- **Fla. Stat. 320.02(5)(a)**: Expressly permits insurers to provide proof-of-purchase cards in **electronic format**.
- Drivers may display digital insurance cards on smartphones or other devices.

**Current Minimum Requirements (2025):**
- **$10,000** Personal Injury Protection (PIP) -- mandatory
- **$10,000** Property Damage Liability (PDL) -- mandatory
- Bodily Injury Liability is **not generally required** (Florida is a no-fault PIP state)
- **Exception**: Bodily injury liability (10/20) is required if the driver has been convicted of DUI or has had their license suspended.

**Pending Legislation -- HB 1181 (2025):**
- Would **repeal Florida's No-Fault / PIP system** entirely.
- Would increase minimum bodily injury liability to **$25,000/$50,000**.
- If enacted, proof of BI liability coverage would be required at vehicle registration beginning July 1, 2026.
- This represents a fundamental shift from no-fault to tort-based system.

**Lienholder Requirements:**
- Lienholders require comprehensive and collision coverage.
- Lienholder listed as loss payee.
- Insurer must provide cancellation notice to lienholder.

**State-Specific Forms:**
- **FR-44 (Certificate of Financial Responsibility)**: Unique to Florida (and Virginia). Required after **DUI conviction**. Certifies coverage at enhanced limits:
  - $100,000 per person / $300,000 per accident bodily injury
  - $50,000 property damage
  - Must be maintained for **3 years** from the date of license suspension.
  - Filed electronically by the insurer with the Florida DHSMV.
  - Average filing fee: ~$15; reinstatement fee: $150-$500.
- **SR-22**: Used for non-DUI financial responsibility violations. Certifies minimum PIP and PDL coverage on a 6-month non-cancellable basis. Must be maintained for 2 years.

**Key Statutes:**
- **Fla. Stat. 320.02**: Vehicle registration and proof of insurance requirements.
- **Fla. Stat. 324.023**: FR-44 financial responsibility requirements for DUI.
- **Fla. Stat. 324.031-324.032**: General financial responsibility requirements.
- **Fla. Stat. 627.736**: PIP coverage requirements.

### 4.3 Commercial Lines

**Standard COI Forms:**
- **ACORD 25, 27, 28**: All standard forms in use.

**COI Regulatory Framework:**
- Florida **does not have a standalone COI statute** comparable to Texas Chapter 1811 or Ohio Chapter 3938.
- General insurance fraud provisions under Fla. Stat. Chapter 626 (agents and adjusters) and Chapter 817 (criminal fraud) apply to falsified COIs.
- **Fla. Stat. 627.4137**: Requires insurers to disclose policy information (coverage limits, policy details) upon written request from claimants. Courts can strike policy defenses as a sanction for non-compliance. (This is a disclosure statute, not a COI-specific statute.)
- The Florida Office of Insurance Regulation (FLOIR) has not issued specific bulletins restricting COI content as of this writing.

**Additional Insured Practices:**
- Standard industry practice in Florida construction and commercial contracts.
- Common endorsements: CG 20 10 and CG 20 37.
- No Florida-specific statutory mandate on additional insured endorsement form or content.

## 5. Texas

### 5.1 Personal Lines -- Home Insurance

**Standard Proof of Coverage Documents:**
- **ACORD 27 (Evidence of Property Insurance)**: Standard for personal lines property.
- **Declarations Page**: Commonly required by mortgage lenders.
- **Insurance Binder**: Temporary proof during closing process.
- **Texas DEC-1 Form**: Texas-specific declarations form used by some carriers.

**Mortgage Company / Lender Requirements:**
- Standard mortgagee clause and loss payee designation.
- Replacement cost coverage required per lender.
- Cancellation notice to lender -- typically 30 days.
- Texas law does not mandate homeowners insurance, but mortgage contracts universally require it.

**State-Specific Considerations:**
- **Windstorm/Hail Coverage**: In designated coastal areas (Tier 1), the **Texas Windstorm Insurance Association (TWIA)** provides wind/hail coverage as the insurer of last resort. Separate wind policy may be required.
- **TDI-Regulated Forms**: Per Texas Insurance Code Chapter 1811, any certificate of insurance related to property coverage must use **TDI-approved forms** (see Section 5.3 below).

**Regulatory References:**
- **Texas Insurance Code, Chapter 2301**: Property and casualty insurance rate regulation.
- **TDI Home Insurance Guide**: tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb025.html

### 5.2 Personal Lines -- Auto Insurance

**Standard Proof of Coverage Documents:**
- **Standard Proof of Motor Vehicle Liability Insurance Form**: Per Transportation Code Section 601.081. Must include:
  - Name of insurer
  - Policy number
  - Policy period
  - Name and address of each insured
  - Policy limits or statement of compliance with minimum requirements
  - Make and model of each covered vehicle

**Electronic Proof of Insurance:**
- **Transportation Code Section 601.053(2-a)**: Drivers may exhibit an image on a **wireless communication device** as proof of financial responsibility.
- **TexasSure Program** (Transportation Code Chapter 601, Subchapter N): Statewide electronic insurance verification system. Law enforcement and DMV offices use TexasSure to verify insurance status in real time.
  - A peace officer **may not issue a citation** for lack of insurance unless the officer attempts to verify through TexasSure and is unable to confirm coverage.
  - If TexasSure shows no coverage, the operator is **presumed** to be in violation.

**Minimum Liability Requirements:**
- **$30,000** bodily injury per person
- **$60,000** bodily injury per accident
- **$25,000** property damage per accident
- Referenced as "30/60/25."

**Lienholder Requirements:**
- Comprehensive and collision coverage required by lienholders.
- Lienholder listed as loss payee.
- Insurer must provide written cancellation notice to lienholder prior to cancellation.

**State-Specific Forms:**
- **SR-22 (Certificate of Financial Responsibility)**: Required for DUI, driving without insurance, and other serious violations.
- Texas does **not** use the FR-44 form.

**Key Statutes:**
- **Transportation Code Section 601.051**: Requirement to establish financial responsibility.
- **Transportation Code Section 601.053**: Evidence of financial responsibility (including electronic proof).
- **Transportation Code Section 601.081**: Standard proof form requirements.
- **Transportation Code Section 601.191**: Penalties for operating without financial responsibility.

### 5.3 Commercial Lines -- Texas Insurance Code Chapter 1811

Texas has the **most detailed COI statute** in the nation: Insurance Code Chapter 1811, Certificates of Property and Casualty Insurance.

**SUBCHAPTER A -- GENERAL PROVISIONS**

**Section 1811.001 -- Definitions:**
- "Certificate of insurance" = a document, instrument, or record (including electronic) executed by an insurer or agent and issued to a third person not a party to the insurance contract, as a statement or summary of P&C insurance coverage.

**SUBCHAPTER B -- PROHIBITED ACTS AND PRACTICES**

**Section 1811.051 -- Altering, Amending, or Extending Coverage:**
- An insurer or agent **may not** issue a COI that alters, amends, or extends coverage or terms provided by the referenced policy.
- A COI **may not** convey contractual rights to a certificate holder.
- A COI **may not** contain a reference to legal or insurance requirements in a contract other than the underlying insurance policy (e.g., no references to construction contract requirements).

**Section 1811.052 -- Use of Approved Forms Only:**
- No insurer or agent may issue a COI unless the form has been **filed with and approved by TDI** under Section 1811.101, or is a standard form deemed approved under Section 1811.103.
- No person may execute, issue, or require issuance of a COI for risks in Texas unless the form is approved.

**Section 1811.053 -- Alteration of Approved Forms:**
- No person may alter or modify a TDI-approved COI form unless the alteration is approved by TDI.

**SUBCHAPTER C -- FORM APPROVAL**

**Section 1811.101 -- Filing and Approval:**
- All COI forms must be filed with the Commissioner.
- A filed form is **deemed approved** after 60 days unless the Commissioner issues an order of approval or disapproval.
- Required language: "for information purposes only" or similar.
- If the form lacks this exact phrase, the Commissioner may still approve if the form states that the COI does not confer rights or obligations beyond the policy, and that policy terms control.

**Section 1811.103 -- Standard Forms Deemed Approved:**
- ACORD forms and other nationally recognized standard forms are generally deemed approved without individual filing.

**SUBCHAPTER D -- ENFORCEMENT**

**Section 1811.201 -- Commissioner Authority:**
- Commissioner may issue cease and desist orders and impose administrative penalties.

**Section 1811.203 -- Civil Penalty:**
- Willful violation: **civil penalty up to $1,000 per violation**.
- Commissioner may also seek **injunctive relief**.

**What CAN Appear on a Texas COI:**
- Job or contract numbers (for identification only)
- Information that exactly mirrors policy language
- Notice provisions if the certificate holder is named in the policy
- Statements that the policy contains blanket endorsements (additional insured, waiver of subrogation)
- Additional insured designation only if an endorsement actually names the holder
- Waiver of subrogation only if an endorsement actually names the holder

**What CANNOT Appear on a Texas COI:**
- References to insurance requirements in contracts other than the underlying insurance policy
- False or misleading information
- Language broader than actual policy terms
- Non-standard terms for the coverage type
- Blanket statements like "no limitations or exclusions for residential construction"
- Supplemental questionnaires or affidavits
- Coverage terms unavailable under the referenced policy type

**Key Statutes:**
- **Texas Insurance Code, Chapter 1811** (entire chapter)
- **TDI FAQ**: tdi.texas.gov/certificates/faq.html
- **TDI Contact for COI Questions**: PropertyCasualty@tdi.texas.gov

## 7. Additional Insured & Endorsement Practices

### 7.1 Common Endorsements (All States)

These endorsements are standard across all four states for commercial lines:

| Endorsement | Description | When Required |
|-------------|-------------|---------------|
| **CG 20 10** | Additional Insured -- Owners, Lessees or Contractors (Ongoing Operations) | During active work/operations |
| **CG 20 37** | Additional Insured -- Owners, Lessees or Contractors (Completed Operations) | After work completion; covers post-completion claims |
| **CG 20 26** | Additional Insured -- Designated Person or Organization | When specific entity needs AI status |
| **CG 24 04** | Waiver of Transfer of Rights of Recovery Against Others (Waiver of Subrogation) | When contract requires waiver of subrogation |

**Key Points:**
- Both CG 20 10 **and** CG 20 37 are typically needed for full additional insured protection (ongoing + completed operations).
- The most current ISO editions are the **12/19 versions**.
- Coverage for the additional insured is limited to liability arising out of the named insured's work -- it does not cover the additional insured's own negligence (in most forms).
- Being listed as a "certificate holder" alone confers **no rights** -- actual additional insured status requires an endorsement to the policy.

### 7.2 Primary & Noncontributory Endorsement

- **CG 20 01 04 13** or equivalent language.
- Makes the named insured's policy respond first (primary) before the additional insured's own coverage, and does not seek contribution from the additional insured's policy.
- Commonly required in construction contracts in all four states.

### 7.3 State-Specific Notes on Additional Insured

- **California**: Civil Code Section 2782 voids indemnity clauses in construction contracts requiring indemnification for the indemnitee's sole negligence. This indirectly affects additional insured requirements (AI endorsements cannot provide broader indemnity than the contract permits).
- **Texas**: Per Chapter 1811, a COI can state that an additional insured endorsement exists, but **only if** the endorsement has actually been added to the policy. The COI itself cannot create AI status.
- **Ohio**: Per ORC 3938.04, a COI cannot alter or extend coverage -- AI status must exist in the actual policy.
- **Florida**: No specific statutory restriction; standard industry practice applies.

## 8. Recent Legislative Trends (2023-2026)

### 8.1 Multi-State COI Legislation Trend

As of 2026, approximately **25+ states** have enacted some form of COI legislation. The trend is toward:
1. Prohibiting COIs from altering, amending, or extending policy coverage.
2. Requiring that COIs are for informational purposes only.
3. Penalizing both the issuer AND the requestor of non-compliant COIs.
4. Voiding COIs that violate the statute.

### 8.2 State-Specific Recent Changes

**California (2023-2026):**
- **SB 1107 (2022, effective Jan. 1, 2025)**: Raised auto insurance minimum limits from 15/30/5 to 30/60/15. First increase since 1967. Relevant because proof-of-coverage documents must now reflect the new minimums.
- **AB 226 (2025)**: FAIR Plan Stability Act -- enhanced financial backstop for the FAIR Plan. Affects home insurance evidence of coverage in wildfire-prone areas.
- No new COI-specific legislation enacted.
- **CDI Commissioner Bulletin 2023-1**: Guidance on SB 1107 implementation for insurers.

**Ohio (2023-2026):**
- No amendments to ORC Chapter 3938 since its 2016 enactment.
- The Certificates of Insurance Act remains in full effect.
- Ohio Department of Insurance continues enforcement through the Division of Fraud and Enforcement (hotline: 800-686-1527).

**Florida (2023-2026):**
- **HB 1181 (2025, pending)**: Proposes repealing the no-fault PIP system and requiring bodily injury liability at 25/50 minimums. If enacted (effective July 1, 2026), this will fundamentally change auto insurance proof-of-coverage requirements.
- **Ongoing property insurance market reforms**: Multiple bills addressing property insurance availability and affordability (SB 2A, 2022 special session; HB 837, 2023 tort reform). While not COI-specific, these affect the underlying coverage that COIs reference.
- No new COI-specific legislation enacted.

**Texas (2023-2026):**
- Texas Insurance Code Chapter 1811 remains the governing statute, unchanged since its 2011 enactment.
- TDI continues active enforcement of COI form approval requirements.
- **TDI continues to publish guidance** at tdi.texas.gov/certificates/ and maintains an FAQ for agents and insurers.
- No amendments to Chapter 1811 during 2023-2026.
